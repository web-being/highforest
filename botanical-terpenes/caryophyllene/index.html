β-Caryophyllene

C15H24 Sesquiterpene

The eleven Boiling points Do terpenes do anything?

After the clove tree, once called Caryophyllus aromaticus. The old name is printed on the plate. A nine-membered ring fused to a four-membered one, a rare arrangement in nature.

Scent
peppery, woody, warm, gently spicy
Found in

What has been studied So far: animals

  • AnimalsIt binds the CB2 receptor, part of the body’s own cannabinoid system, without the intoxicating effects linked to the CB1 receptor. In a 2014 study, mice fed caryophyllene showed less inflammatory and nerve pain, and the effect depended on CB2.4
  • ReviewA 2020 review summarizes mostly preclinical work on chronic inflammation.3
  • Not knownWhether this holds in people. The work cited here was done in mice and in cells.
